Foundational Problem

The Failure of Scalar Probabilistic Confidence

Modern generative language models evaluate epistemic validity through token probability likelihoods or post-hoc scalar confidence metrics: $P(\text{valid} \mid x) \in [0, 1]$. In low-stakes conversational applications, this scalar abstraction is sufficient. However, in high-consequence regulatory, international trade, and structural engineering environments, scalar confidence fails fundamentally.

✕ Output-Centric Generative AI (Flaw)

Scalar Confidence Conflation: Collapses absence of evidence, contradictory statutory evidence, and aleatoric noise into a single numerical score.

  • Sycophantic alignment with user presuppositions
  • Silent hallucinations on out-of-distribution queries
  • Inability to represent active statutory conflict (Consensus ≠ Truth)
✓ Stateful Epistemic Control (AEI)

Multidimensional Epistemic State: Replaces scalar probabilities with a discrete, verifiable control tuple tracked across time.

  • Explicit EPISTEMIC_SILENCE boundary gates
  • Dialectical tension detection between competing statutes
  • Provable audit trail before generating binding legal affidavits

The Epistemic State Tuple Formulation

In Adaptive Epistemic Intelligence, any proposition or regulatory calculation $\psi$ is governed by a state vector $\mathbf{E}(\psi, t)$:

E(ψ, t) = ⟨ s, ΔFD, Δ²FD, DT, τ, Φ ⟩

Where s ∈ S represents the discrete operational state, ΔFD is the Fringe Divergence derivative against validated canons, DT ∈ [0, 1] is the Dialectical Tension (degree of unresolvable statutory contradiction), τ is the temporal validity horizon, and Φ is the cryptographically pinned provenance chain.
